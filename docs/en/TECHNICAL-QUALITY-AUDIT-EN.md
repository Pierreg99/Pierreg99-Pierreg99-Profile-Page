## 4. Quality vs Cost Model

For 2026 DACH planning, published market guidance places software-development pricing broadly around **€90–€150/h for established agencies**; DACH freelancer medians are approximately **€95–€105/h**, depending on the dataset and definition. A current German full-stack reference gives roughly **€75–€140/h**, with a €100/h median. These are market-planning figures, not a valuation of Pierreg99.

For comparability, the team model uses a normalized **€100/h blended-rate assumption**.

| Model | Typical Composition | Relative Quality Potential | Cost Multiplier | Best Fit |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Solo Generalist | 1 developer | 6.5–8.0 | **1.0×** | MVPs, prototypes, focused tools |
| Senior Full-Stack | 1 senior developer | 8.0–9.0 | **1.2×** | Complex applications with concentrated ownership |
| 2-Person Expert Pod | Senior full-stack + specialist | 8.5–9.3 | **1.7×** | AI + web, 3D + web, product acceleration |
| 3-Person Full-Stack Team | Lead + full-stack + QA/FE | 8.7–9.5 | **2.5×** | Product delivery with review and QA separation |
| 5-Person Specialist Team | Lead + FE + BE + AI/Data + QA | 9.0–9.7 | **4.2×** | Larger production products |
| Enterprise Squad | 6–10+ incl. DevOps/PM/Security | 9.2–9.9 | **6×+** | Regulated or SLA-heavy systems |

### Value-for-Cost Interpretation

The strongest economic signal is **breadth per contributor**: one capable developer can cover areas that may require several specialists in a conventional organization. This does not imply that one person replaces a mature production organization. Review capacity, security, operations, UX research, product management and parallel delivery remain team advantages.
